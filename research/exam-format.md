# BASI Comprehensive Global Program — Exam Format Research

Researched 2026-10-05. Confidence labels:
- **[Official]**: stated on a basipilates.com / basiglobal.com / BASI support page.
- **[Licensee]**: stated by a regional BASI licensee or host studio. These are official for that region, but details vary by country.
- **[Student]**: a first-hand student account or a student-made flashcard deck.
- **[Inferred]**: my own reading of the sources above. Not stated outright anywhere.

## 1. Exam structure

### Certification requirements (Comprehensive) [Official]
- Attend all 12 modules. Flex students instead do Flex Modules 101–108, watch the pre-recorded lectures, and pass the lecture quizzes at ≥70%.
- Pass **two online multiple-choice exams** (Midterm and Final) at **≥70%**. Both are taken in the BASI Portal.
- Log **500 student hours**: 100 observation, 200 student teaching and 200 self-practice. Up to 50% of observation hours can be done online.
- Submit a **Student (research) Paper**. Listed on host/licensee pages and in the Newport Beach centralized-testing listing.
- Pass the **Final Evaluation ("Centralized Testing")**, which has two parts:
  - **Teaching Evaluation**: a one-hour Comprehensive session for a volunteer client, "following the BASI Comprehensive Block System and using repertoire taught within the course modules".
  - **Practical Exam**: demonstrate **4** movements (in-studio) or **10** movements (self-recorded online). The movements are chosen at random from Modules 1–12 (Flex 101–108).
- In-studio clients are assigned. They are **intermediate level with minimal to no physical restrictions**. For the self-recorded option you supply your own client.
- Results are posted in the Portal within 30 days and cannot be appealed. If you fail one part, you retake only that part (additional fee). Online written exams "may be retaken immediately until they are passed."

Sources:
- https://www.basiglobal.com/examination-process
- https://www.basipilates.com/education/exam-process/
- https://support.basiglobal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/centralized-testing-information
- https://www.basipilates.com/faq/basi-pilates-education/

### Written exams
- Format: **multiple choice, online, 70% pass mark** [Official].
- **Number of questions** [Licensee, BASI Germany/Austria/Switzerland/Scandinavia]:
  - Module 4 quiz.
  - Midterm (Module 7): about **75 MCQ** on Modules 1–6.
  - Final: about **100 MCQ**.

  Source: https://www.basipilates-natax.net/deutschland/ausbildung-und-termine-deutschland/educational-overview-cttc-without-mttc-austria/
- **Durations** [Licensee, BASI UK, seen only in a search snippet because the page would not load]:
  - Module 4 written quiz: 30 min.
  - Midterm: 1 h 30 min.
  - "2 Hour written exam" at Module 12.

  Source: https://www.basipilatesuk.com/education/comprehensive-program
- **Open book with a time limit**: reported only for the *Mat* program's online exam [Student]. Source: https://jenniejwalker.substack.com/p/training-to-become-a-pilates-instructor. Whether the Comprehensive exams are open book is **unconfirmed**.
- **Content** [Inferred from the official module descriptions and student flashcards]. The flashcards are student-made, not BASI material:
  - Pilates history: born Düsseldorf 1883, NYC 1926, "Contrology".
  - The **10 BASI movement principles**: Awareness, Balance, Breath, Concentration, Center, Control, Efficiency, Flow, Precision, Harmony.
  - Neutral pelvis and neutral spine definitions.
  - Breathing mechanics and lateral breathing.
  - **Definitions of "muscle focus" vs "objectives"**.
  - Anatomy: prime movers for spine, hip, knee and ankle actions; planes and axes; joint types; contraction types.
  - Posture: plumb line landmarks and postural deviations.
  - **Block System order and contents**.
  - The order of exercises within series, e.g. the Reformer Footwork sequence.
  - Exercise setup and breath patterns.
  - Contraindications. Module 5 covers contraindications; Module 12 covers pregnancy and special populations.
  - Training principles (overload, specificity, reversibility).
  - Exercise physiology.

  Sources:
  - https://quizlet.com/145319266 ("Pilates BASI Written Exam", 154 cards)
  - https://quizlet.com/870562576/basi-final-exam-flash-cards/ (126 cards, mostly muscle actions)
  - https://basipilatesasia.com/training-schedule/comprehensive-teacher-training-program/ (module topics)
- **No official sample questions found.** Whether the written exam asks for exact spring settings or rep counts is **not confirmed**.

### Practical exam
**What examiners look at** [Official, from the BASI support article "Teaching Evaluation and Practical Exam Expectations"; the page itself is JS-only, so the text comes from the search-index copy]. Pass mark is **≥70% on a rubric**. The criteria are:
1. Proper **setup** for each exercise.
2. Coordination of **movement and breath**.
3. **Control, precision, and flow**.
4. Familiarity with the equipment, **including correct spring settings** and safe use.

Source: https://support.basiglobal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/teaching-evaluation-and-practical-exam-expectations

**Mix of exercises** [Licensee, BASI Pilates Academy Newport Beach]: 4 movements, of which **1 is Mat and 3 are apparatus**. The pool includes Avalon/F2 repertoire. Source: https://goteamup.com/p/4959169-basi-pilates-newport-beac/courses/73685/

**Regional variants** [Licensee]:
- Germany/Austria/Switzerland/Scandinavia: the midterm practical has 3 exercises. The final practical is 4 exercises **plus a group mat class in which you teach 2–3 exercises**.
- UK: the Module 7 midterm includes a practical of 4 random exercises.

**Higher bar for comparison** [Official]: BASI's optional "Certificate of Excellence" (examined by Rael Isacowitz) has a 1 h teaching session plus 6 exercises (2 Mat, 4 apparatus). Its focus is "setup, performance, precision, breath pattern, and flow", and the pass mark is 90%. Source: https://www.basipilates.com/education/%F0%9F%8C%9F-certificate-of-excellence-in-person-assessment-at-basi-hq/

**Duration**: no official figure.
- One student reported the combined teaching and practical final as "2 hour" [Student]. That student passed teaching and failed the practical. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bwalya-joyce-mwape-94a96931_pilates-vivacore-growthmindset-activity-7485302746066259968-xUuY
- The Germany licensee schedules the final exam on the Module 12 date for students who have completed ≥50% of their hours or received a positive faculty evaluation.

### Teaching evaluation rubric [Official, same support article]
1. Presentation, attitude, preparedness.
2. Verbal, visual and **tactile** cueing.
3. Observe, analyze and correct.
4. **Time management and sequencing using the BASI Block System**.
5. Client engagement and a session appropriate to the client's level.
6. Equipment safety protocols and familiarity with the equipment.
7. Precision in the BASI method and tailoring to the individual.

Additional detail [Licensee]:
- Newport Beach: "Structure of the session is at the discretion of the student, but must be limited to repertoire taught within the modules." Graded pass/non-pass.
- Germany/Austria: the session must use **at least two pieces of equipment**.

## 2. What to know per exercise

BASI's own manuals (the Study Guide and the movement-analysis workbooks for Mat, Reformer, Auxiliary, Cadillac and Wunda Chair) are not public. The closest public proxy is Rael Isacowitz's *Pilates* (Human Kinetics, 3rd ed. 2022). Its introduction says each exercise lists [Official-adjacent, author = BASI founder]:
- **level of difficulty** (Fundamental / Intermediate / Advanced),
- **recommended resistance range** (springs: Light / Medium / Heavy),
- **number of repetitions**,
- **muscle focus**,
- **objectives**,
- commentary,
- **imagery**,
- a checklist of key points ("keys to success").

The 3rd edition also adds **modifications** (easier), **variations** (same difficulty) and **progressions** (harder). "Assists" are external aids such as springs, bands and cushions.

Sources:
- Scribd preview of the 3rd-ed. intro: https://www.scribd.com/document/1061837329/Instant-Access-Pilates-3rd-Edition-by-Rael-Isacowitz-ISBN-1492598860-978-1492598862
- https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/adapting-exercises-while-preserving-the-legacy-of-pilates
- Google Books index terms ("Muscle Focus", "Objectives", "Imagery", "RESISTANCE Light Medium Heavy", "Setup"): https://books.google.com/books/about/Pilates.html?id=HlNIEAAAQBAJ
- Germany licensee note on the Study Guide and five workbooks: https://www.basipilates-natax.net/deutschland-en/education-and-dates/cttc-comprehensive-teacher-training-course-studio-und-mattenausbildung/

The student flashcards follow the same pattern:
- **block**, **starting position**, **movement with inhale/exhale per phase**, **muscle focus**, **objectives**.
- Example: Pelvic Curl. Muscle focus: abdominals and hamstrings. Objectives: hamstring control, pelvic-lumbar stabilization, spinal articulation.
- They also test the **order of exercises within a block or series**.

Sources: https://quizlet.com/318272117 · https://quizlet.com/533632364

How each detail is supported:

| Detail | Evidence it matters for the exam |
|---|---|
| Spring setting | **Confirmed**: in the practical rubric ("correct spring settings"). In Isacowitz's book as a Light/Medium/Heavy range. BASI Systems colours: Red = heavy, Blue = medium, Yellow = light; gears also change tension (https://pilatesology.com/2025/04/what-reformer-springs-do-i-use/). |
| Setup (headrest, footbar, straps, box, gear) | **Confirmed**: "proper setup" is rubric item 1. Rael: "The setup is 90% of the exercise" (https://www.pilatesanytime.com/workshop-view/3136/video/Pilates-Balance-Control-Front-Deconstructed-by-Rael-Isacowitz). BASI also runs a "Set Up For Success" workshop. |
| Breathing pattern | **Confirmed**: rubric item 2. Every flashcard lists inhale/exhale per phase. |
| Flow / tempo / control | **Confirmed**: rubric item 3 ("control, precision, and flow"). No source states a numeric tempo. |
| Block placement and order within block | **Confirmed for teaching** (sequencing via the Block System) and heavily drilled in flashcards. **Inferred** to appear on the written exam. |
| Repetitions | In the book and manuals. Exam questions on it **not confirmed** [Inferred]. |
| Level (Fundamental/Intermediate/Advanced) | In the book. Relevant to the teaching eval, where the client is "intermediate" [Official]. |
| Muscle focus / objectives | Defined terms in the BASI manuals. In flashcards and on the written exam [Student]. |
| Modifications / contraindications / special populations | Taught in Modules 5, 6 and 12 [Official]. Likely on the Final written exam [Inferred]. |
| Cues / imagery / tactile cueing | **Confirmed** for the teaching eval rubric. |

## 3. How the Block System is used

- It is a "filing system" that orders exercises for "progressive structuring of classes within a flexible framework" [Official]. Source: https://www.basipilates.com/innovation/basi-block-system/
- It is introduced in Module 3 [Official]. Source: https://www.basipilates.com/education/teacher-training-programs/comprehensive-teacher-training-program-usa/foundation/

**Mat blocks (6)**, as in the book chapter order and the Quizlet deck:
1. Foundation (Warm-up)
2. Abdominal Work
3. Spinal Articulation
4. Bridging
5. Lateral Flexion/Rotation
6. Back Extension

**Comprehensive / apparatus blocks (12)**, from student flashcards and a Dutch studio page:
1. Warm-up
2. Foot Work
3. Abdominal Work
4. Hip Work
5. Spinal Articulation
6. Stretches
7. Full Body Integration I (Fundamental/Intermediate)
8. Arm Work
9. Leg Work
10. Full Body Integration II (Advanced)
11. Lateral Flexion/Rotation
12. Back Extension

**Sources disagree on where Leg Work sits.** Quizlet places it *after* FBI II. Reformerise places it *before* FBI II. Check this against your manual. A known mnemonic from a midterm deck groups the blocks as "Works For All" (Warm-up, Foot, Abdominal), "HSS" (Hip, Spinal articulation, Stretches), "FAF" (Full body 1, Arm, Full body 2), "LLB" (Leg, Lateral flexion/rotation, Back extension). That puts Leg Work after FBI II.

Sources:
- https://quizlet.com/87715460/basi-block-system-flash-cards/
- https://reformerise.nl/pilates/basi-block-system/
- Docsity midterm preview: https://www.docsity.com/en/docs/basi-midterm-exam-questions-and-answers-already-graded-aor-updated-and-verified-or-2024/12540185/

**Use in the exam** [Official]: the teaching evaluation is a one-hour session for an intermediate client, sequenced by the Comprehensive Block System. Examiners score "sequencing exercises properly using the BASI Block System" and time management. The Mat-only self-recorded test is a 30-min Mat session following the Block System.

**Multi-apparatus session** [Inferred, plus Licensee]: the session is expected to use more than one apparatus (Germany/Austria requires ≥2). A published BASI student paper shows a typical shape:
- Warm-up on the Mat.
- Foot Work → FBI → Arm Work on the Reformer.
- Leg Work, Lateral Flexion and Back Extension on the Wunda Chair.
- A Roll-down cool-down.

Source: https://studyres.com/doc/2456325/research-paper-for-basi-comprehensive-teacher

**Flexibility of block order**: one Mat graduate writes that blocks "don't have to go in a certain order" [Student]. Every official description, however, stresses a logical sequence. Follow the standard order in the exam [Inferred].

## Bottom line for study design
- **Practical**: for every exercise in Modules 1–12 on every apparatus, be able to:
  - set it up yourself (springs, gear, footbar, headrest, straps, box),
  - run the breath pattern,
  - perform it with control and flow.

  Expect 1 Mat exercise and 3 apparatus exercises (in-studio), or 10 exercises (self-recorded).
- **Teaching**: build a one-hour multi-apparatus intermediate session in Block System order, and drill your timing.
- **Written**: know the principles, anatomy, Block System order and contents, series order, muscle focus and objectives, breath patterns, and contraindications and special populations. Springs and reps are worth knowing, but whether they are tested there is unconfirmed.
